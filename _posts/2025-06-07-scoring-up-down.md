# Educator Life
I've been an educator for a long time. Tutoring, Teaching school, Lecturing, so I've made my share of Exams. I weighted the pros and cons of multiple choices vs essay, Open Book vs Close Book, Take home or in Class. I've read some guidelines on how to make these quizes. Somehow I haven't thought of whether I'm scoring up or down. Tbh I haven't fully wrapped my head around what it means in practice and how will the question change. How to standardize it or even which is the better phylosophy. 

I think I've done both (inadvertently) and I can say I prefer the scoring up method. 
- in Engineering Drawing I do scoring down. Whenever a student draws a line that is not up to standard that is -3 or -5. Its very difficult to stay perfect.
- Most exams I do scoring up. and this way I get to put the max score to be 120.
- Curve grading might be the worst. It might promote competition but in my experience everyone just scores "0" therefore you pass by scoring "0".

With scoring down, you assume that the lecturer have successfully taught the student therefore the baseline is 100. Then with every "gotcha" the students' score gets deducted. With scoring up, you assume the student knows nothing but they can freely express what they know about the subject. Isn't it harder for the student if they're assumed to know everythere therefore its impossible to "underpromise and overdeliver"?

As always its gotta be something in the middle. You assume the kid is a mid, then you have some questions that proves the students know more than needed, and some questions that proves that the students know less than needed. While this system might work well in like math, and exercisse idk how it'd work in other subjects.
